I recently - well - not that recently (about a year ago) - switched from a job where I worked 60+ hr weeks to one where I work a standard 40 hours.  Since the company is EST  and I live in PST, Im basically free after 2pm and have a lot of time on my hands.  I've taken this chance to learn new programming languages, and work on a few hobby projects more seriously.  Ive also brought some of my old projects back from the dead and given them a little facelift - namely my old Starcraft bot.  Anyways - I think the time has come to start writing up some of these projects and showing them off - to that end, my New Years resolution was to write one post a month.  Well - as you can see, its not going too well, but better late than never I guess.  

I think Ill start by posting a quick writeup I did on my first experiences w/ the [Julia](https://julialang.org/) language using it to code out an implementation of RSA from scratch.  I will say - as I was writing that post - I found it getting a little long and unfocused.  Does a reader really want to see my fumbling attempts at showing why optimizations like Carmichaels totient and the Chinese remainder theorem are useful and important?  Is it really useful to explain Miller-Rabin?  I think I might have to cut it down and may just focus on the basics of the algo and its goals.  We'll see.

I think next I'll probably write up something on using powerful ML estimators like Xgboost or sklearns MLP implementation as a Kaplan Meier estimator for survival analysis.  Its a problem that comes up surprisingly often, and I've found the already implemented algorithms in the excellent [liflines](https://lifelines.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) package are too slow for the kind of rich datasets you encounter in a professional context.

After that .... I'm not sure.   I made a fun little AI for the popular [2048](https://2048game.com/) game to beat my brother's high score.  That was pretty cool, I think I finally understand alpha/beta pruning now - as well as the importance of a good hueristic function and why OpenAI used machine learning for their hueristics. 

I also spent a lot of time working on a VR physics/animation engine for [Babylon.js](https://www.babylonjs.com/) for a Avatar game.  That project needs a ton of work though, and I actually had to stop working on it for a bit b/c I ran into a version conflict b/w the packages I was using.  I'll probably come back to it later, but to be honest I dont know when I could demo it on this blog.

I might write up an analysis of CDC vaccination and Vaers data I did after a discussion w/ a couple friends where I realized that we couldnt actually back up anything we were saying w/ hard facts.  I do worry that by the time I get around to that writeup Ill have to redo all the work, and since Im not a SME I just worry Ill make some kind of dumb mistake on a topic people care a lot about.  

I may also revisit a topic I researched in college and have returned to off and on ever since.  Every year EIA and Lazard publish LCOE #s for various power sources, and whenever they are discussed online I see a lot of confusion about how the numbers are calcuated and what they mean.  I also see people post a lot of #s from source specific orgs like IRENA and IAEA, and do all kinds of apples to oranges comparisons w/ them.  I think I could do a ton of posts about either one of those topics, but I might just start out w/ a simple replication of LCOE #s and some discussion about input parameters and assumptions.  Again, I wonder when Ill get around to this, just because this is a topic people seem to get very emotional about and I really want to get the calculations right.


Well - I think thats all I have on the horizon for the immediate future - lets see what I can get done this year.
