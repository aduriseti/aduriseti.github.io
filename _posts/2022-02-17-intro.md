I recently - well - not that recently (about a year ago) - switched from a job where I worked 60+ hr weeks to one where I work a standard 40 hours.  Since the company is EST  and I live in PST, Im basically free after 2pm.  Now that I have some time on my hands, Ive been taking my hobby projects a little more seriously.  I've also brought some of my old projects back from the dead and given them a little facelift - namely my old Starcraft bot.  Anyways - I think the time has come to start writing up some of these projects and showing them off.  

I think Ill start by posting a quick writeup I did on my first experiences w/ the [Julia](https://julialang.org/) language using it to code out an implementation of RSA from scratch.  I will say - as I was writing that post - I found it getting a little long and unfocused.  Does a reader really want to see my fumbling attempts at showing why optimizations like Carmichaels totient and the Chinese remainder theorem are useful and important?  Is it really useful to explain Miller-Rabin?  I think I might have to cut it down and may just focus on the basics of the algo and its goals.  We'll see.

I think next I'll probably write up something on using powerful ML estimators like Xgboost or sklearns MLP implementation as a Kaplan Meier estimator for survival analysis.  Its a problem that comes up surprisingly often, and I've found the already implemented algorithms in the excellent [liflines](https://lifelines.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) package are too slow for the kind of rich datasets you encounter in a professional context.

After that .... I'm not sure.   I made a fun little AI for the popular [2048](https://2048game.com/) game to beat my brother's high score.  I think I finally understand alpha/beta pruning now - as well as the importance of a good hueristic function and why OpenAI used machine learning for their hueristics. Also I think an a post w/ an embedded widget of my AI autoplaying the game would be cool.

I also spent a lot of time working on a VR physics/animation engine for [Babylon.js](https://www.babylonjs.com/) for an Avatar game.  That project needs a ton of work though, and I actually had to stop working on it for a bit b/c I ran into a version conflict b/w the packages I was using.  I'll probably come back to it later, but to be honest I dont know when I could demo it on this blog.

I might write up an analysis of CDC vaccination and VAERS data I did after a discussion w/ a couple friends where I realized that it was difficult to find any objective hard analyses of effect size online.  I do worry that by the time I get around to that writeup Ill have to redo all the work to incorporate the much larger amount of data gathered from then till now, and since I'm not a SME I just worry Ill make some kind of dumb mistake on a topic people care a lot about.  

I may also revisit a topic I researched in college and have returned to off and on ever since.  Every year EIA and Lazard publish LCOE #s for various power sources, and whenever they are discussed online I see a lot of confusion about how the numbers are calcuated and what they mean.  I also see people posting a lot of #s from source specific orgs like IRENA and IAEA, and making all kinds of apples to oranges comparisons.  I think I could do a ton of posts about either one of those topics, but I might just start out w/ a replication of LCOE #s and some discussion about input parameters and assumptions.  Again, I wonder when Ill get around to this, for some reason its a charged subject and I dont want to make any mistake in my implementation.

Well - I think thats all I have for the forseeable future --- looking forward to what I can get done by EOY.
